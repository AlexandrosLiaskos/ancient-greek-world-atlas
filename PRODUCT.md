# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The atlas serves people exploring the geography and historical structure of the ancient Greek world: culturally curious visitors, students, educators, and researchers who need to move quickly between map context and trustworthy records. They may arrive with a known place in mind or browse comparatively across settlements, sanctuaries, colonies, and polities. On any screen, their primary task is to find, compare, locate, and understand entities without losing the provenance or uncertainty behind the data.

## Product Purpose

The product turns a reviewed bilingual corpus of 226 entities into an accessible public WebGIS. It succeeds when a visitor can discover a place through map, catalogue, search, filters, or relationships; understand what the point represents and how certain it is; inspect chronology and sources; switch languages without losing context; and share or revisit the exact state of the atlas.

## Positioning

A bilingual atlas that makes the ancient Greek world geographically explorable without hiding the limits, provenance, or uncertainty of the evidence.

## Brand Personality

Authoritative, exploratory, and restrained. The voice is concise and scholarly without sounding institutional or remote. The interface should feel composed and culturally specific, with a sense of scale and historical depth emerging from the material itself rather than from ornamental “antique” decoration.

## Anti-references

The atlas must not resemble a generic tourism site, a parchment-themed history page, a card-heavy template, or a dense professional GIS dashboard that assumes specialist training. It must not copy only the superficial typography of Lagoons while losing its coherent workbench behaviour. Redundant subtitles, decorative catalogue numbering, numbered individual markers, detached legends, oversized filter blocks, and mobile layouts that leave no usable catalogue space are explicitly excluded.

## Design Principles

1. Keep map and catalogue equally trustworthy ways into the same corpus.
2. Make classifications, counts, chronology, and uncertainty immediately legible before adding decoration.
3. Treat Greek as the original interface language and English as a complete parallel experience.
4. Reveal scholarly depth progressively: fast discovery first, full evidence on demand.
5. Preserve spatial and interaction context across filtering, record navigation, sharing, and mobile use.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The public interface targets WCAG 2.2 AA. Every workflow must be keyboard and touch operable, maintain visible focus, support reduced motion, avoid colour-only distinctions, keep readable contrast and text sizing, announce result changes without noise, and remain usable at narrow mobile widths and 200% browser zoom. Greek polytonic text, long bilingual place names, and right-sized touch targets are first-class layout cases.
